# AI Policy

AI tooling is increasingly part of the software development landscape, Buddies of Budgie being no exception. This document outlines our expectations for transparency, accountability, and quality when/if AI is involved in contributions.

:::info[Why this matters]
Budgie Desktop is used by people around the world as their daily computing environment. Every change has the potential to affect the experience of countless users. We take that responsibility seriously, and contributors (whether using AI tools or not) must do the same.

We take great pride in our work. Writing software is pragmatic by nature. It has to work, be maintainable, and be easy to reason about (at least, as easy as one can reason about an entire desktop environment). But we also treat writing code as a craft. We would rather move deliberately than move fast and break things. That care and intentionality is something we want to preserve in every contribution.
:::

## Our Stance

Buddies of Budgie follows a **human-centric development** model. It is our position that only humans can be **authors** and ultimately be held accountable. _People_ develop for and deliver the Budgie Desktop experience to other _people_, whereas "AI" / LLMs are merely tools used to facilitate our objectives.

We recognize that AI tools like Claude, Gemini, Copilot, opencode, and others can be valuable. They can help explore ideas, work with the author to draft or explore technical implementations, accelerate development, and improve the clarity of communicating everything from blog posts to writing issues and pull requests.

This policy does not endorse any particular model or provider. Our policy intends to draw a clear line between two distinct categories of the Buddies of Budgie software projects.

## Budgie 11

This section / category applies to any codebase built with the intent to be used for Budgie 11 and beyond. Any codebases built with Budgie 11 in mind that also serves Budgie 10 is also in this category.

:::note[Summary]
A high-level summary of our policy section for this category: Use of AI / LLM for code generation is **forbidden** and will be **wholesale rejected**. Any use of AI / LLMs for alternative use cases, such as for research purposes, is not endorsed by the project.
:::

Throughout our development of Budgie 10.10.x, particularly after the release of Budgie 10.10, we have leveraged large language models (LLMs) in: guided feature development (human as the software architect), improvements to code quality targeting long-term maintainability, debugging, and performance analysis. Therefore, we are the first to understand what value these tools _can_ bring when used responsibly.

However, Budgie 11 represents a ground-up rewrite and re-architecture of Budgie Desktop in Qt, C++, and Rust. This not only provides the team the opportunity to properly learn the best practices for the tools and languages involved in its development, but the opportunity to make mistakes as well (_which is equally as valuable in building a corpus of knowledge on how to iterate on Budgie 11_).

By taking into use LLMs, we would be:

- Potentially misguiding ourselves by taking the facts presented by LLMs from research at face value, which could be trained on outdated knowledge / practices (whereas at least with Budgie 10, we have a decade of experience with its codebase and can more easily identify bad practices)
- Taking shortcuts in its implementation but not in our learning, creating a body of work that may not be thoroughly understood
- Onboarding risk in a fresh codebase when it comes to licensing

Therefore, it is the policy of the Buddies of Budgie team that **no components** associated with Budgie 11 (including budgie-desktop-services, which is used for both Budgie 10.10.x and Budgie 11), shall include any code generated by "AI" / large-language models.

:::info
No codebase within this category has included code generated by an AI / LLM as of the updating of this policy on October 17th, 2026, nor will include any going forwards. Budgie 11 should be considered **"AI-free"**, at least when it comes to first-party codebases.
:::

While we cannot control how one uses these tools beyond code generation, we will not encourage its use for research purposes either. Rather, it would be preferable to build out a comprehensive list of suggested readings and authoritative sources of documentation that can / should be used for reference instead, as well as leveraging or building out tooling ourselves to facilitate various use cases (such as performance analysis, isolated development environments, etc).

:::note[Philosophy on "AI-free" Code]
We do not, nor wish to, impose our requirement for "AI-free" code on any software projects we leverage. We understand and respect that other developers may make different choices or similar to us, are conflicted on the subject. As such, given the prolific nature of AI tooling, it is certain that Budgie 11 will or already has ended up leveraging software (e.g. toolkits or libraries) that have taken into use such tooling. The organization, nor Budgie 11, should not be viewed as "anti-AI". It is simply that for our specific desired objectives with Budgie 11 and the way we wish to develop it, we have decided to draw a clearer line in the sand on its use (or rather lack thereof).
:::

As we cannot predict the future and how these types of tools will evolve, we feel the need to add the disclaimer that while this restriction is not _likely_ to be revisited, we do open the door to it being done so should _at minimum_ **all** of the defined criterium is fulfilled:

1. Budgie 11 has been seen its first stable release
2. Copyright law (or cases influencing it) in all jurisdictions of the members of the "Best Buds" clearly establishes that code generated by LLMs may be correctly licensed to the project's intended license(s)
3. If/when such tooling or models can be trained and implemented in an ethical manner that guarantees the copyrights and licenses of all involved parties is respected and satisfied. Preferably in a local-first implementation as well

## Non-"Budgie 11"

This section / category applies to the following. Unless otherwise communicated, anything not listed below should be considered under the scope of "Budgie 11":

1. Any codebase or project designed with the intent to be used _strictly_ for Budgie 10 (budgie-backgrounds, budgie-desktop, budgie-control-center, budgie-desktop-view 10.10.x, budgie-session)
2. Our documentation center
3. Our website

A high-level summary of our policy section for this category: Use of AI / LLM for code generation is **permitted**, so long as it is:

1. Thoroughly validated and tested before submission per our [Testing Requirements](#testing-requirements)
2. Appropriately attributed per our [Attribution guidelines](#attribution)
3. Involves human-in-the-loop and audited. Simply saying "keep me in the loop" does not count. You must have an understanding of the proposed fix, collaborate on architectural decisions (or make them fully yourself), and not simply leave critical decisions up to the model
4. Would not otherwise be viewed as "slop" by the respective reviewers (subject to change or vary based on the reviewer or at review time)
5. Meet our standards for code quality, practices, and balanced documentation

In this specific category, contributors are _generally_ free to make their own informed choices about which tools they use, however end-to-end agentic workflows is soundly rejected as it would violate our human-centric development model and "human-in-the-loop" requirement.

A codebase such as the documentation center or website may be subject to be moved to Category One, should they be fully expunged of any generated code by an LLM. In practice, this would mean a full black-box rewrite.

### Code Contributions

#### Testing Requirements

All contributions must be tested before submission. When AI tools are involved, this is especially important as AI-generated code can be subtly wrong in ways that look correct at first glance.

- **Non-trivial AI-assisted code** must be built, installed, and tested by the contributor
- **Substantial changes** (refactors, new features, behavioral changes) require rigorous, thorough testing
- The contributor must verify the changes work as intended in a running Budgie Desktop session where applicable

:::tip[What counts as "trivial"?]
"Trivial" is intentionally loosely defined. Typo fixes, documentation corrections, comment updates are considered trivial. If you're unsure whether your change qualifies, err on the side of testing it. When in doubt, build and install.
:::

#### Attribution

When AI tools assisted with **code changes** (writing, modifying, or generating code), add an `Assisted-by` trailer to the commit message with the tool and model identifier. This is in addition to the requirements outlined in our [Contributing](/developer/contributing) documentation:

```
feat(panel): add workspace indicator tooltip

Implement hover tooltips showing workspace names in the workspace
indicator applet.

Assisted-by: Claude:claude-opus-5-5 (1M)
Signed-off-by: Your Name <your.email@example.com>
```

##### Format

```
Assisted-by: <Tool>:<model-id>
```

##### Examples

| Tool           | Trailer                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Claude         | `Assisted-by: Claude:claude-opus-5-5 (1M)` |
| Gemini         | `Assisted-by: Gemini:gemini-2.5-pro`       |
| GitHub Copilot | `Assisted-by: Copilot:gpt-4o`              |
| ChatGPT        | `Assisted-by: ChatGPT:gpt-4o`              |

##### When Attribution Is Required

- AI wrote or generated code that ended up in the commit
- AI substantially modified or refactored existing code
- AI generated an implementation that the contributor then adapted

##### When Attribution Is NOT Required

- AI was used for **research only** - asking questions, understanding APIs, exploring approaches - but the human wrote all the code
- AI performed purely **mechanical tasks** like formatting, running commands, or committing (e.g. using any `commit` skill we may have)
- AI was used to help **write documentation** for issues or pull requests (not committed code)

:::note
The distinction is about code in the repository. If you used AI to understand how GTK layer shell works and then wrote the implementation yourself, no trailer is needed. If AI generated the implementation and you reviewed/adapted it, include the trailer.
:::

### Pull Request Guidance

When submitting a pull request that includes AI-assisted code:

- Ensure all relevant commits include the `Assisted-by` trailer and align with our [Contributing](/developer/contributing) documentation
- Ensure an Assisted-by trailer is included at the end of any summary section of the work (before any section for tests / test plan)
- Your test plan should reflect that you actually built, installed, and verified the changes
- Supplemental comments on the PR with AI prompt/planning information are welcome and encouraged for research and learnings, but not mandatory

:::info[Sharing AI context]
If you used an AI agent for planning or implementation, sharing that context as a **PR comment** can be valuable for reviewers and for the project's collective learning. However, AI planning documents should **not** be committed to the source repository. Share them as PR comments instead.
:::

### Blog Posts and Written Content

The attribution requirements in this policy are specific to source code contributions. Blog posts and other written content are a different matter. Writing is inherently a process of drafting, editing, and refining, and AI tools can be a natural part of that workflow.

AI tools may be used to assist with writing blog posts, release announcements, and other project communications. No `Assisted-by` attribution is required for written content. The author is still expected to review, refine, and stand behind what is published; the final voice should be theirs.

Content that is wholly generated by AI tooling without meaningful human involvement is never acceptable. Readers should never expect that a blog post or any form of written content by Buddies of Budgie was produced entirely by AI. There is always a human involved in the process of shaping the narrative, ensuring accuracy, and making it their own.

## Enforcement

:::danger
Intentional obfuscation of AI tooling usage is grounds for rejection and may result in being blocked from future contributions.
:::

The goal of these LLM related enforcements are to enable us to have honest, transparent collaboration.

The enforcement happens when an AI Policy violation is discovered (at the time of contribution or later):

1. On the first violation you will be provided a warning unless the violation is deemed severe enough by a member of "Best Buds" (for example en masse PR submissions, clear fully agentic submissions)
2. After one warning, any future violations, will cause you to be blocked from future contributions
3. When a violation occurs your contribution can be rejected, reverted or rewritten

This enforcement is not subject to debate. At the end of the day, we are all volunteers that must review the contribution, take into consideration existing and future users or use cases, and more.

## This Policy Applies Equally

This policy is tool-agnostic. It applies to Claude, Gemini, Copilot, ChatGPT, locally-hosted models, and any other current or future models. The expectations are the same regardless of which tool you use.

## Living Document

This policy is subject to change as the AI landscape and our own experience with these tools evolve. Contributors are expected to be mindful of updates to this policy and to periodically revisit it to ensure their practices remain in alignment.
